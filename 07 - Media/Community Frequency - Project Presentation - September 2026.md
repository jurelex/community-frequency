# Community Frequency — Project Presentation

**Researching whether affordable mesh communications can help connect rural and geographically isolated communities in Peru.**  
**September 2026**

Community Frequency is an independent research project focused on a specific question: whether affordable, low-power mesh communications can provide useful communications for rural and geographically isolated communities in Peru where conventional cellular or Internet infrastructure may be limited or unavailable.

This is not a general mesh deployment project. **Peru is the reason for the research; mesh communications are the technology being evaluated.**

---

## 1. The Problem We Are Trying to Understand

In rural Andean and rainforest regions of Peru, geographic isolation can leave communities outside conventional communication infrastructure.

Limited cellular coverage, Internet access, power, and difficult terrain can make communication much harder.

For communities already facing economic and geographic disadvantages, lack of connectivity can become another barrier to opportunity, education, emergency assistance, health services, and connection with the outside world.

**Community Frequency begins with that community problem.**

## 2. The Question

The technical question is specific:

> **Can inexpensive, low-power mesh networking provide useful communications without requiring access to commercial cellular or Internet networks?**

The project is not assuming that the answer is yes.

The purpose of the research is to determine whether mesh can provide meaningful value in the environments and circumstances being studied.

## 3. Why Mesh?

Mesh networking is the technical approach currently being investigated.

In a mesh network, distributed devices can communicate with one another and, where the technology supports it, relay information through other nodes.

**Person A → Node → Node → Node → Person B**

Actual performance depends on geography, terrain, node placement, antennas, configuration, power, technology, and radio conditions.

That is why Community Frequency is testing and measuring rather than assuming.

## 4. Why Peru?

Peru is not simply a convenient test location.

The research is specifically concerned with rural and geographically isolated communities in Peru, including environments in the Andes and rainforest where conventional communications infrastructure may be limited.

The diversity of Peru also creates different research conditions. What works in an urban environment may not work in a mountain or rainforest community.

**The project therefore needs evidence from Peru itself.**

## 5. Step Zero — Peru 2026

The first major research mission has three objectives.

### 1. Establish the regulatory baseline

Research Peruvian telecommunications requirements and seek meetings or interviews with the Ministerio de Transportes y Comunicaciones (MTC).

The goal is to understand what equipment, frequencies, power levels, antennas, and configurations can legally be used.

### 2. Learn and test in Lima

Meet members of the Peruvian mesh community, exchange practical experience, compare Meshtastic and MeshCore, and conduct initial testing where practical.

### 3. Test in northern Peru

Travel to a community near the Peru–Ecuador border and evaluate the technology in a real rural environment.

The detailed mission plan is maintained separately in the Peru 2026 research documentation.

## 6. Research Before Deployment

Community Frequency is not beginning with a deployment plan and looking for evidence to justify it.

The sequence is:

**Research → Test → Observe → Document → Learn → Decide**

Before any larger community project is considered, we need to understand:

- What is legally possible.
- What equipment is available and practical.
- How the technology behaves in relevant environments.
- What the actual communication needs are.
- What the costs and maintenance requirements look like.
- Whether the technology provides meaningful value.

If the evidence does not support a larger deployment, that is a useful research result.

## 7. Learning in Lima

Lima provides an initial opportunity to learn from people already involved in mesh communications in Peru.

The project will exchange experience with the local mesh community and test both Meshtastic and MeshCore where practical.

The comparison will consider factors such as coverage, reliability, multi-hop behavior, configuration, power requirements, and operational usability.

The goal is not to declare a winner. It is to understand which approach, if either, may be appropriate for the problem being studied.

## 8. Testing in Northern Peru

The northern Peru work is intended to move the research from an urban learning environment into a real rural community context.

We want to observe:

- Actual communication needs.
- Terrain and coverage.
- Node placement and elevation.
- Multi-hop performance.
- Reliability.
- Power requirements.
- Ease of use and maintenance.
- Whether the technology provides meaningful value to the community.

**The field environment is part of the research, not merely a backdrop for a technology demonstration.**

## 9. What We Plan to Evaluate

The initial equipment target is intended to give the project enough flexibility to investigate different configurations.

It includes:

- 2 × BQ G3 nodes.
- 2 × external antennas for the BQ G3s.
- 4 × Heltec V4.
- 2 × Heltec T114.
- 4 × GPS/GNSS kits for V4/T114.
- 2 × Seeed Studio Solar Pro 2 nodes.
- 6 × Muzi Works 915 MHz antennas.
- 6 × batteries.
- Cables, connectors, mounting hardware, and field accessories.

This is a target, not equipment already acquired or approved for operation. Regulatory research will determine what can legally be used in Peru.

## 10. Building the Equipment Ourselves

Where practical, enclosures, mounts, brackets, and other mechanical parts will be 3D printed.

The purpose is to keep equipment inexpensive and adaptable rather than relying entirely on proprietary enclosures and accessories.

The project will document useful hardware and field configurations so successful approaches can be reproduced or improved later.

## 11. What Would Make the Research Useful?

A successful test is not simply one that achieves a long radio range.

We need to learn whether a system can be:

- Reliable.
- Affordable.
- Low-power.
- Practical to deploy.
- Usable by people who are not radio engineers.
- Maintainable locally.
- Appropriate for the terrain.
- Useful for the community's actual needs.

A result showing that a technology does not meet those requirements is still useful.

## 12. How the Amateur-Radio Community Can Help

The research benefits from people who have practical experience.

Clubs, operators, and technical contributors can help with:

- RF, antennas, and propagation.
- Meshtastic and MeshCore experience.
- Power and portable systems.
- Field-testing methods.
- Experimental design.
- Equipment and loaners.
- Connections to people with relevant rural or international experience.
- Critical review of assumptions and test methods.

**The goal is not simply to gather equipment. It is to build better evidence.**

## 13. Why Funding Matters

Community Frequency is an independent project.

Equipment must be acquired before the field research can be conducted. Funding can help provide the nodes, antennas, GPS/GNSS hardware, batteries, accessories, and materials needed for the research.

The target equipment list represents what we would like to take into the field. Funding will determine how much of that kit we can actually acquire.

## 14. What Comes After Step Zero?

Step Zero is intended to establish a factual baseline.

We want to return from Peru knowing:

- What is legally possible.
- What hardware is practical and available.
- What works in Lima.
- What works in the field.
- How Meshtastic and MeshCore compare in these environments.
- What the communities actually need.
- What the real costs and maintenance requirements look like.

Only then can we make an informed decision about whether a larger community project makes sense.

## 15. The Research Record

Detailed experiment logs, configurations, measurements, observations, photographs, and limitations will be documented in the project's technical research record.

The public website provides the context and methodology. The technical record provides the evidence.

Transparency matters because the value of this project is not only what works. It is also what we learn when something does not work.

## 16. An Invitation

Community Frequency is looking for people who want to help understand a real communications problem.

You may have experience with radio, antennas, propagation, mesh networking, solar power, field deployments, rural communications, community projects, or simply a perspective that challenges an assumption.

That experience can help shape the research.

**Research the need. Test the technology. Let the evidence shape the solution.**

## 17. The Vision

The long-term question is simple:

Can communities that are geographically isolated from conventional communications gain an additional, practical communications option using technology that is affordable, low-power, maintainable, and appropriate to their environment?

We do not know the answer yet.

That is why we are researching it.
